# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in PuppyClicker for Minecraft welcoming, safe,
and equitable for everyone. Technical experience is not a condition of
respect: people asking basic questions, reporting confusing behaviour, or using
assistive technology are as entitled to patience and dignity as experienced
contributors.

## Encouraged behaviour

- Be kind, honest, and mindful of how words may be understood across different
  cultures, backgrounds, and communication styles.
- Respect different viewpoints, identities, boundaries, and experiences.
- Give and receive constructive feedback without personal attacks.
- Take responsibility for mistakes and work to repair harm.
- Protect credentials, private information, and informed consent.
- Focus discussion on the project and the well-being of its community.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, or unwanted personal attention.
- Insults, character attacks, trolling, or discriminatory conduct.
- Sexualised behaviour or imagery inappropriate to the project space.
- Publishing private information or credentials without permission.
- Impersonation, deliberate misinformation, or evasion of moderation.
- Encouraging unsafe, non-consensual, or automatic physical-device actions.
- Other conduct that threatens a welcoming, safe, and collaborative community.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to repository issues, pull requests, discussions,
reviews, project email, and other spaces managed by the project. It also applies
when someone is officially representing the project in public.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report a possible violation privately to
[minecraftclicker@eim.ie](mailto:minecraftclicker@eim.ie). Do not open a public
issue about another person's conduct.

Reports will be handled as confidentially as practical. The maintainer may
remove or edit content, issue a warning, limit participation temporarily, or
ban someone permanently depending on the seriousness and pattern of the
behaviour. Decisions will prioritise safety, fairness, context, and the
possibility of repairing harm.
